Philosophy
Why prototype first

Most games fail not because of bad code, but because the core mechanic was never actually fun. This lab exists to kill bad ideas fast — and double down on the ones that create that "one more go" pull.

Every prototype here is stripped to its minimum viable fun. No story, no polish, no audio — just the mechanic, naked and honest. If it doesn't work here, it won't work with a $200K budget.

The goal isn't to ship prototypes. The goal is to learn what makes a player lean forward instead of put the phone down.

The core question

"Does this mechanic make the player want to play again — without being asked to?"

Build order
What to build first and why

Resources are finite. Here's the sequencing logic — each step informs the next.

01
Echo City
The primary hypothesis. Sound-first design is genuinely novel in mobile — no major competitor owns this space. If the mechanic lands, the differentiation is structural, not cosmetic.
02
One Button Chaos
Tests surprise-as-mechanic. If players stay engaged when the rules change every level, that's a reusability signal — content updates become mechanic updates, not asset updates.
03
The Game That Lies
Tests subverted tutorial design. Players who laugh when they die are players who share. This is a virality probe as much as a mechanic test.
04
Boss Rush Dodger
Pattern-reading is evergreen. Validates whether your audience skews toward reflex-based or puzzle-based play — critical data for Echo City's difficulty curve.
05
Reaction Heist
The stealth timing test. If this scores highest on Day-2 return, the audience wants deliberate gameplay — which shapes Echo City's pacing and ping-rate decisions.
